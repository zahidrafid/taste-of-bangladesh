Spices of a Bengali Kitchen

The flavors that make Bangladeshi food taste the way it does.

Panch Phoron: The Five-Spice Mix

Panch phoron means "five spices" in Bengali. It is a mix of five whole seeds in equal amounts. Unlike most spice mixes, it is used whole, not ground into powder. The seeds are fried in hot oil for a few seconds until they pop, and then the vegetables, fish or lentils are added.

The five seeds in panch phoron
English Name Bengali Name
Fenugreek seedMethi
Nigella seedKalo jira
Cumin seedJira
Black mustard seedShorshe
Fennel seedMouri

Everyday Spices

  • Turmeric (holud): gives curries their yellow color.
  • Cumin (jira): used whole or as a powder in almost every curry.
  • Coriander (dhone): the powder goes in curries, and the fresh leaves are used on top.
  • Dried red chili and chili powder (morich): for heat.
  • Bay leaves (tej pata): added to hot oil at the start of cooking.
  • Mustard (shorshe): used as seeds, as a paste for fish, and as mustard oil.

Mustard Oil

Mustard oil has a strong, sharp smell and taste. It was the traditional cooking oil in Bangladesh before soybean oil became common. Today many people still use a little raw mustard oil to flavor bhorta (mashed dishes) and to fry hilsa fish.
