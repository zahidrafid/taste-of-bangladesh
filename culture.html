Food & Festivals

In Bangladesh, every celebration comes with its own special food.

Pohela Boishakh (Bengali New Year)

Pohela Boishakh is the first day of the Bengali calendar. In Bangladesh it is celebrated on April 14 with colorful fairs, music, and a big parade in Dhaka called Mongol Shobhajatra.

The traditional breakfast on this day is panta ilish: rice soaked in water overnight (panta bhat), served with fried hilsa fish, green chilies, onion and different kinds of bhorta.

Winter Pitha Season

Pitha are traditional rice cakes. The pitha season starts in late autumn, when farmers harvest the new rice. In winter (December to February) you can find street sellers making fresh pitha on almost every corner of Dhaka, and many towns hold pitha festivals (pitha utshob).

The most common winter pitha is bhapa pitha, a steamed rice flour cake filled with coconut and date molasses (khejur gur). Other popular kinds are chitoi pitha and patishapta.

Ramadan and Eid

Iftar

During Ramadan, families break their fast at sunset with iftar. Popular items are piyaju (lentil fritters), beguni (fried eggplant), chola (spiced chickpeas), jilapi and dates.

Eid ul-Fitr

On Eid morning, many homes serve shemai (sweet vermicelli cooked in milk) and other sweets to family and guests.

Eid ul-Adha

On this Eid, families share meat with relatives, neighbors and people in need. Beef and mutton curries are the main dishes.
